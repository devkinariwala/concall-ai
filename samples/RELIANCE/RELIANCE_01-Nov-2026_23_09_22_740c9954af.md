## Notable Analyst Q&A

### Probal Sen (ICICI Securities) - *(Page 21)*
- **Q:** Inquired about the timeline for the normalization of the 5 million barrels of refinery capacity impacted in Russia and the Middle East, estimating 6 to 9 months in a best-case scenario or longer.
- **A:** Management stated that uncertainty is very high due to continuing attacks in the Strait of Hormuz and low inventories, and they do not see an immediate change in the situation.
  > "So is it fair to assume this 5 million barrels is probably six to nine months away from normalization in a best case scenario, and worst case it could be it could stretch for even longer?"

### Probal Sen (ICICI Securities) - *(Page 22)*
- **Q:** Asked how management is positioning the crude sourcing mix given increased risk and uncertainty of supplies despite improving flows from Saudi Arabia and the Middle East.
- **A:** Management explained that they swung dramatically between East of Suez and West of Suez on a month-to-month basis in Q2 and will continue to look for opportunistic crude sourcing in the next quarter.
  > "How are you positioning the sourcing mix, is there a realignment back to Middle East flows because the product deal obviously is fairly different for Middle Eastern based crude for your refinery versus let us say a US-based or an Africa-based crude"
  > "I think during the presentation I mentioned that we swung between East of Suez and West of Suez quite dramatically in Q2 itself from month-to-month."

### Jal Irani (Nuvama) - *(Page 23)*
- **Q:** Inquired whether structural demand destruction in China due to rapid adoption of EVs and LNG as fuel would lead to permanently lower refining margins.
- **A:** Management acknowledged China's structural shift to EVs and LNG trucks, but noted that demand growth in the rest of the world will offset it, and historical market memories of supply shocks will prevent normalisation of margins and freight.
  > "I am just wondering is there a structural destruction of demand and if we go back to a normalized level will we actually see refining margins lower than the previous normalized levels"

### Sanjay Mookim (J.P. Morgan) - *(Page 24)*
- **Q:** Requested an update on the commissioning timeline for various new energy lines including solar value chain and batteries.
- **A:** Management stated that the solar module capacity is over 6 GW (reaching 20 GW early next year) and solar cell is nearing 5 GW (reaching 20 GW in 1H next year). BESS facility will commission in the next half-year of the calendar year, and cell ramping will start between June and December.
  > "Can we get a quick update on the commissioning of the various new energy lines, please?"
  > "I think we are well on target as what we had earlier indicated with the commissioning."

### Vikash Jain (CLSA) - *(Page 27)*
- **Q:** Asked whether online grocery growth is underwhelming compared to quick commerce players and if the target of doubling EBITDA in two years remains confident, as well as inquiring about O2C spread capture improvements.
- **A:** Management reaffirmed the target to double EBITDA over two years, noting that growth is pursued in a measured, sustainable way leveraging existing infrastructure and a 400-million customer base rather than pure cash burn. For petrochemicals, ethane benefits will increase with incremental vessels, and relaxed LPG control orders helped improve volumes compared to the previous quarter.
  > "So, on retail, the revenue growth number for online grocery that has been given about 46% or something this if we compare it to the biggest quick commerce players also, it is still underwhelming, to say the least."
  > "We are not changing ours that is the target that we are going for. There is no reason, at least as of now, for us to change that target."
  > "I will speak from the petrochemicals perspective. So, you mentioned ethane benefit is here to stay, and it will only go up as we absorb the incremental vessels."

## Risks & Uncertainties

- **[Macroeconomic | Management Acknowledged]** Turbulence in the macro environment and disruptions in the energy sector. *(Page 3)*
  > "exceptionally strong quarter in a turbulent world and turbulence in macro, turbulence because of energy disruption."

- **[Macroeconomic | Management Acknowledged]** Inflation is at a two-year high, impacting consumer purchasing power. *(Page 7)*
  > "Inflation is on a two year high, which is impacting the purchasing power for people."

- **[Macroeconomic | Management Acknowledged]** Significant currency depreciation is impacting the cost of imported products and landed product prices. *(Page 7)*
  > "Currency has depreciated significantly. That is impacting, especially, some of the imported products. The landed products have gone up."

- **[Macroeconomic | Management Acknowledged]** Monsoon deficit has caused demand pressure across key agrarian-dependent states and rising food prices are eating into discretionary demand. *(Page 7)*
  > "Monsoon deficit. So that has caused some demand pressure across some of the key agri dependent states, as well as the food prices are moving up. So that is also eating into the discretionary demand."

  > "there seems to be a significant amount of demand destruction especially in China they are moving to EVs very rapidly and to LNG as a fuel, now I am just wondering is there a structural destruction of demand"
