## Management Commentary

- The bank's branch distribution has doubled over the last 5 years and will start to mature as they move into the next 5 years. *(Page 6 | **Speaker:** Sashidhar Jagdishan)*
  > "As I had mentioned, the kind of rigor that the institution is undertaking in terms of building better customer interactions and engagement and trying to step up new account acquisitions. The fact of the matter is a lot of our distribution, which has doubled over the last 5 years, will start to mature as we move to the next 5 years."

- HDFC Bank sees better slippage outcomes across most of its portfolios rather than it being a function of loan mix. *(Page 12 | **Speaker:** Srinivasan Vaidyanathan)*
  > "We are seeing better across most of these portfolios on the slippages, so it's not mix."

- Over the last 3 years, HDFC Bank has substituted borrowing maturities of erstwhile HDFC Limited of almost INR2.6 lakh crores, necessitating more time deposits. *(Page 13 | **Speaker:** Sashidhar Jagdishan)*
  > "It's just that, you know, the balance sheet is so large. And the fact of the matter is that over the last 3 years, we have had to substitute the borrowing maturities of the erstwhile HDFC Limited, which is almost nearing about INR2.6 lakh crores, which necessitated the institution to take more time deposits, substituting that."

## Notable Analyst Q&A

### Mahrukh Adajania (Tara Capital Partners) - *(Page 6)*
- **Q:** Management was asked when the retail deposit proportion would increase, what the impact of drought on agri and small business loans would be, and if there are strategy changes in HDFC Life.
- **A:** Management explained that branch distribution has doubled and will mature, extraordinary outflows in large deposit segments are currently masking core momentum, and agri sowing remained stable with no gross NPA deterioration.
  > "So when do you see the proportion of retail deposits inching up now? Because it's come off a bit over the last few quarters."

### Kunal Shah (Citi) - *(Page 8)*
- **Q:** What is driving the NIM reduction to 3.32 despite lower sequential corporate book growth, and what is driving the sequential uptick in interest expense and cost of funds?
- **A:** Management clarified that cost of funds was flat quarter-on-quarter at 4.4%, but higher time deposit growth and FCNR deposit mix offset repricing benefits, while corporate bank grew 2.8% sequentially and 16.4% year-on-year.
  > "So, when we look at it, in fact, the corporate book growth is not as substantial sequentially as we see with the other banks, but the impact on NIMs for us has almost been similar in terms of the quantum"
  > "So, if you look at the deposit growth that we have had, 1.57 trillion, almost 1.3 trillion is the growth in time deposit. So, that outweighs from an overall cost mix point of view"

### Rikin Shah (IIFL Capital) - *(Page 9)*
- **Q:** How are FCNR funds being deployed, how much will retire wholesale borrowings, and at what incremental yields are funds being invested in money markets?
- **A:** Management stated that FCNR deposits are deployed as soon as possible to obtain ANBC benefits for PSL purposes, and sources and uses are largely matched within the quarter.
  > "I wanted to understand how are you deploying the FCNR funds? How much of it will be used to retire wholesale borrowings?"
  > "Rikin, the FCNR deposits, you can take it as deployed because we do need to deploy it as soon as possible. That is why you will get the benefit of the ANBC in the following cycle for comparison for PSL purposes."

### Piran Engineer (CITIC Securities) - *(Page 12)*
- **Q:** Why did HDFC Bank cut its MCLR much more than peers in the last rate cycle, and can the same be expected in a rate hike cycle?
- **A:** Management noted that MCLR operates on certain rate dynamics existing on the last day of the prior period, incorporates cost to assets and credit costs, and that the proportion of the book priced to MCLR is minuscule single digits.
  > "Now, in the last rate cycle, HDFC Bank cut their MCLR much more than ICICI and Axis. Just wanted to understand the reasons behind that and in a rate hike cycle, could we expect the same?"

  > "The full-quarter impact of the FCNR will be a headwind that will offset."

  > "Now, when you put all this together, structurally, I think this is going to be an issue in the system over a period of time."
